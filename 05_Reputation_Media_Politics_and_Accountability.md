# Reputation, Media, Politics, and Accountability

## 1. Reputation Structure

The city uses a composite reputation score with category subscores.

The player should see:

- Exact numerical contributions
- Plain-language explanations
- The direction of change
- Major contributing events

Possible categories include:

- Public safety
- Service reliability
- Business confidence
- Government credibility
- Fiscal management
- Development climate
- Transparency
- Resident trust
- Emergency readiness

## 2. Reputation Timing

Most reputation changes occur gradually.

Immediate swings are reserved for:

- Major disasters
- Landmark achievements
- Highly publicized negligence
- Major court rulings
- Severe corruption or accountability events
- Exceptional emergency response

## 3. Local Media

Local media amplifies the spread and consequences of city information.

Media can report:

- Department failures
- Successful projects
- Court rulings
- Emergency response
- Developer disputes
- Public complaints
- Budget decisions
- City achievements
- Historical events

Media does not create the underlying event, but it affects how quickly and broadly the event influences the city.

## 4. Media Tiers

Media reach develops through separate facility types.

### Newspaper

- Slower information spread
- Primarily local reach
- Lower immediate reputation effect
- Stronger long-form or historical reporting role

### Radio

- Faster citywide spread
- Stronger short-term effects
- Useful during emergencies
- Broader daily influence

### Television

- Rapid and broad coverage
- Largest reputation swings
- Strong visual impact
- Greater political pressure

Each tier requires its own physical building.

They are not upgrades to one generic Media Center.

## 5. Multiple Outlets

Multiple outlets of the same type may exist.

There is no fixed one-newspaper, one-radio, one-television maximum.

More outlets can increase:

- Coverage speed
- Story persistence
- Competition
- Public awareness
- Scrutiny
- Conflicting narratives

## 6. Private Media Development

Private media companies can request development approval for:

- Newspaper offices
- Radio stations
- Television studios

These are handled through the broader private-development proposal system.

The city reviews:

- Land use
- Site suitability
- Infrastructure
- Traffic
- Building compliance
- Development terms

The city does not approve or reject the outlet based on preferred editorial bias.

## 7. Freedom of Speech and Editorial Independence

Once approved, a private outlet remains editorially independent.

The city is at the mercy of freedom of speech.

The city cannot select a private outlet's political position or reporting style before construction.

A private outlet may later become:

- Friendly
- Critical
- Sensational
- Investigative
- Business-oriented
- Public-safety focused
- Inconsistent

These tendencies should emerge from the outlet's own simulated identity rather than city selection.

## 8. City-Owned Media

The city may build and operate its own media outlet.

A city-owned outlet can provide:

- Emergency information
- Public-service announcements
- City explanations
- Official statements
- Counter-messaging

It should not erase private reporting or automatically win public trust.

Its credibility depends on:

- Accuracy
- Transparency
- Past performance
- Government reputation
- Independence safeguards
- Whether it appears propagandistic

## 9. Rejection of Media Development

A single rejection of a media development proposal should not automatically create a major penalty.

Legitimate reasons may include:

- Zoning incompatibility
- Traffic
- Infrastructure limitations
- Site safety
- Incomplete application
- Similar treatment of comparable projects

Repeated rejection of the same development type can create suspicion.

Repeated rejection of media outlets is especially sensitive.

Potential consequences:

- Reduced government credibility
- Increased media scrutiny
- Lower developer confidence
- Reputation for suppressing criticism
- Legal challenge
- Public transparency concerns

The game should examine the city's broader approval pattern rather than treating every rejection as censorship.

## 10. Media and Court Rulings

Major court rulings initially create legal and financial consequences.

Once the city has sufficient media coverage, rulings can also create:

- Reputation effects
- Political pressure
- Business-confidence changes
- Public anger
- Demands for leadership changes
- Historical reporting

## 11. Political Approval Structure

Decision authority is divided.

### City Manager

Can approve:

- Routine purchases
- Standard operations
- Department priorities
- Normal city construction within budget
- Many development decisions

### Mayor

May approve:

- Major purchases
- Politically sensitive decisions
- Large operational changes
- Important appointments

### City Council

May approve:

- Large capital programs
- Debt-funded projects
- Major long-term commitments
- Significant public expansions

## 12. Political Pressure

The Mayor and Council can respond to:

- Reputation
- Media coverage
- Budget stress
- Unequal services
- Emergency failures
- Developer complaints
- Court rulings
- Resident dissatisfaction

The player must manage both actual city performance and the political interpretation of that performance.

## 13. Accountability Record

The game should preserve a structured record of:

- Warnings issued
- Recommendations made
- Information available at the time
- Player actions
- Player acknowledgments
- Unresolved commitments
- Final outcomes

This record supports:

- Court liability
- Media reporting
- Mayor reactions
- Final disaster reports
- Historical city archives

## 14. Future Media Expansion

Deferred possibilities include:

- Outlet-specific biases
- Investigative journalism
- Competing narratives
- Public polling
- Press conferences
- Editorial endorsements
- Media ownership consolidation
- Defamation or unlawful-conduct cases
- Regional or national media
- City communications department upgrades

These are not required for the initial core system.
