# Citizens, Education, Employment, and Neighborhood Risk

## 1. Individual Citizen Simulation

Each citizen has individual statistics rather than existing only as an aggregate population number.

Possible citizen variables include:

- Education
- Employment
- Income band
- Housing status
- Satisfaction
- Trust
- Crime risk
- Litter or garbage risk
- Health and safety exposure
- Location
- Access to services

The city still uses aggregated dashboards, but those dashboards are built from individual outcomes.

## 2. Education Tiers

The proposed education tiers are:

1. Uneducated
2. Secondary
3. Vocational
4. University

Jobs require specific mixes of education levels.

Example warehouse workforce:

- 10 Secondary workers
- 50 Vocational workers
- 3 University workers

Schools may require a large share of Vocational and University workers depending on the facility level.

## 3. Employment Matching

Employers seek workers with appropriate education.

Uneducated employment may include:

- Farming
- Mining
- Oil
- Basic labor

When citizens cannot find appropriate work, they may:

- Become unemployed
- Become homeless
- Leave the city
- Accept work for which they are undereducated

Undereducated workers may create:

- Lower productivity
- Higher accident risk
- Lower service quality
- Slower business growth

## 4. Overeducation

Overeducated workers may accept lower-skill jobs.

Consequences can include:

- Lower satisfaction
- Higher turnover
- Continued job searching
- Migration toward better opportunities

This prevents education from being an automatic universal benefit without economic planning.

## 5. Traditional Schools

Traditional schools educate the correct age range most efficiently.

They should be the fastest path for standard progression.

School capacity matters.

When enrollment exceeds approximately 80%:

- Education speed declines

When enrollment exceeds approximately 95%:

- The penalty becomes much stronger

Understaffing adds another penalty.

## 6. Dropouts and Failure

Severe overcrowding or understaffing may cause:

- Academic failure
- Dropouts
- Delayed progression

Dropouts can later return through the Education Center.

## 7. Education Center

The Education Center supports residents who aged out of the normal school system.

It can be built before a full traditional school network.

Characteristics:

- All-age access
- Flexible enrollment
- Slower education speed
- Secondary education at base level
- Independent Vocational extension
- Independent University extension

The University extension does not require the city to build a separate university first.

## 8. Education and Personal Risk

Education is a major factor in individual risk calculations.

Dropout or educational failure can increase a citizen's probability of:

- Crime
- Litter
- Garbage problems
- Unemployment
- Housing instability

Education is the highest-weight factor in the proposed model, but it is not the only factor.

## 9. Dynamic Risk

Citizen risk changes over time.

Examples:

- Job loss increases crime risk
- New employment lowers crime risk
- Stable housing lowers risk
- Poor services raise risk
- Education lowers risk
- Local disorder raises risk
- Strong community investment lowers risk

Risk should not be permanently attached to a citizen.

## 10. Location Modifiers

Citizen risk interacts with location.

A citizen's behavior can be affected by:

- Nearby crime
- Service access
- Fire exposure
- Garbage conditions
- Housing condition
- Employment access
- Local trust
- Patrol concentration

The result is a location-based heatmap produced from citizen-level data.

## 11. Clustering and Nonlinear Effects

One high-risk citizen should have little citywide impact.

A cluster of high-risk citizens can produce nonlinear amplification.

Possible outcomes:

- Crime hotspot
- Litter concentration
- Fire neglect
- Vacancy
- Reduced investment
- Resident departure

The amplification should be capped so neighborhoods remain recoverable.

## 12. Police At-Risk Mode

Police can use an At-Risk operating mode.

Patrols concentrate around hotspots using a bell-curve distribution rather than placing every officer on one exact tile.

This allows:

- Strongest coverage at the hotspot center
- Reduced but meaningful coverage nearby
- Less abrupt patrol boundaries

## 13. Over-Policing

Keeping At-Risk patrol concentration too high for too long can reduce trust.

The effect is especially strong in lower-income areas.

This creates a tradeoff between immediate suppression and long-term legitimacy.

## 14. Trust Recovery

Trust can recover passively after excessive patrols are reduced.

Recovery accelerates through:

- Education
- Parks
- Outreach
- Reliable services
- Employment
- Infrastructure investment
- Neighborhood improvement

## 15. Multi-Effect Investments

Investments should show several expected effects.

A project may be labeled with:

- High effect
- Medium effect
- Low effect

Example: an Education Center may strongly improve education, moderately improve employment, and slightly improve crime risk.

The game should reveal major relationships without exposing every internal formula.

## 16. High-Skill Employers

Strong education can attract high-skill employers.

These employers may import workers if the local workforce is insufficient.

Imported workers increase:

- Housing demand
- Property values
- Traffic
- Service demand
- Displacement pressure

Education and economic development therefore need to be balanced with housing production and infrastructure.

## 17. Neighborhood Recovery

Neighborhood decline should be reversible.

Recovery may require combinations of:

- Employment
- Education
- Housing repair
- Service improvements
- Infrastructure
- Reduced vacancy
- Appropriate policing
- Parks
- Private investment
- City-owned development

No single building should instantly erase accumulated decline.
